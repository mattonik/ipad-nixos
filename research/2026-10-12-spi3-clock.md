# SPI3 functional clock: 60 MHz, not the 24 MHz placeholder (2026-10-12)

**Evidence (ADT):** the `spi3` node has `clock-ids = 0x12e` (one cell). As for the UART nodes (`research/2026-10-11-uart3-register-capture.md`: `clock-ids {0x115, 0x4}` -> entry 0x15 = 21 = 24 MHz), the low byte indexes the 96-entry little-endian `clock-frequencies` table of the `arm-io,t7001` node: **entry 0x2e = 46 = 60,000,000 Hz** (entries 43-46 are all 60 MHz). Companion entries: 24 MHz at 0/1/3/21/40/47, 100 MHz at 4, 48 MHz at 42.

**Evidence (hardware):** with the device tree's 24 MHz placeholder `clkref` the driver computes `CLKDIV = ceil(24 MHz / speed)` but the controller really runs from 60 MHz, so the real SCK is `60 MHz / CLKDIV`. Measured on T4 (transmit-only, 8 bits per byte, per-byte time includes a small fixed overhead):

| requested | CLKDIV (24 MHz) | measured us/byte | implied SCK | 60 MHz / CLKDIV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 500 kHz | 48 | 6.40 | ~1.25 MHz | 1.25 MHz |
| 1.984 MHz | 13 | 1.74 | ~4.6 MHz | 4.62 MHz |
| 4 MHz | 6 | 0.81 | ~9.9 MHz | 10 MHz |

The match is exact, so the controller clock is 60 MHz and everything SPI3 did up to now ran 2.5 times faster than the label said. Consequences: the word delay (patch 0096, `ceil(ns x clk_get_rate / 1e9)`) was computed for 24 MHz, i.e. 2.5 times too short in real time; all the earlier "1.984 MHz" touch runs were really ~4.6 MHz; the "requested 3.5 MHz" Apple-timing run was ~8.6 MHz.

**Fix:** patch 0100 adds a 60 MHz `fixed-clock` (`clk_spi3`) to `t7001.dtsi` and points `spi3` at it. Requested rates are then real (`CLKDIV = ceil(60 MHz / speed)`) and the word delay register value matches Apple's formula. Apple's own 124 ns clock period (cell 1 of the touch `reg`) is then reachable: 60 MHz / 8 = 7.5 MHz.

## Hardware confirmation (T7 payload, 2026-10-12)

With patch 0100 the measured transmit-only byte time matches the requested clock exactly: 4.14 us/byte at a requested 1.984 MHz (clock alone 4.03), 15.96 at 500 kHz (16.00), 1.08 at 7.5 MHz (1.07), and the word delay is a true 5.0 us per byte (6.07 vs 1.07 us/byte at 7.5 MHz). The full 59,288-byte packet at Apple's real wire timing (7.5 MHz requested, 5 us word delay, 360 ms) and at 7.5 MHz without delay and 1.984 MHz with delay (541 ms) all still return `4c b1`: the ROM reply is unaffected by true clock and delay.
