# J81 touch: post-discovery mode write tested, contact IRQ still absent (2026-11-02)

The live J81 result has passed HBPP detection, firmware ACK (`4bc1`), touch-calibration ACK, register writes, execute, N1 discovery, and input-device registration. In run Z1e, continuous contact caused no new ATN interrupts or input events. That puts the current failure before the contact parser. See `research/2026-11-01-orchardlinux-org-review.md`, runs B1 and Z1b–Z1j.

## Concrete difference: the J81 Z2FW recipe is not Apple's bootload order

Apple's J81 `MTSPIBootloader_Z2::bootloadDevice()` sends **firmware, proximity calibration, touch calibration, register calibration, calibration request, execute** (the kernelcache trace in `research/j81-z2-n1-transport-framing.md` and `research/2026-10-11-touch-no-ack-analysis.md`). The J81 personality gives touch calibration address `0x10009000` and proximity address `0x10009600` (`docs/plans/2026-09-09-j81-touch-spi3.md`).

Orchard's `src/orchard/apple/z2packet.py:convert()` instead emits `CONFIG, touch CALIBRATION, firmware SEND`. There is no proximity record. This is inherited from the working **J71t/iPad 5** recipe; it is not evidence that the order is correct on J81. The J81 ADT contains a 128-byte `prox-calibration` property (all zero in the captured unit's ADT), and the Z1 DT had `apple,z2-prox-cal-blob`. The manual B1 run sent firmware then touch calibration, but also omitted proximity calibration; contacts were not tested in B1. The two runs therefore have not tested Apple's full J81 sequence.

**Live test, 2026-11-02:** The user booted the J81 touch payload and confirmed touching during each capture. We rebuilt `touch-diag` because the existing `/tmp/touch-diag` lacked `hold`, then staged the already-built `spi-apple.ko` and `apple_z2.ko`. A private Z2FW with `CONFIG, firmware SEND, prox CALIBRATION at 0x10009600, touch CALIBRATION at 0x10009000` preserved the original firmware packet byte-for-byte. The DT supplied the 128-byte zero proximity blob and 1024-byte unit touch blob. The driver bound, registered `iPad5,3 Touchscreen`, discovered controller `0x434d15c1`, firmware `0x381`, sensor `40x30`, and surface `14745x19657`. Its calibration status was `0x4969`; it emitted `0x50` status frames. During a 45-second confirmed touch window, IRQ 57 (`apple-z2-irq`) stayed at 2 and `/dev/input/event1` yielded no bytes. Thus Apple's calibration order plus proximity calibration is **insufficient** to start contact reporting on this unit. It should not be copied into the shared J71t converter on this evidence.

**Synchronized repeat:** At the user's request we reloaded the same `0x381` firmware and calibration wrapper, waited for their “ready,” then announced the start of a 60-second capture. The user confirmed dragging a finger for most of the window. `/dev/input/event1` produced **0 bytes** and IRQ 57 stayed at its post-boot total of **6** (5 on CPU0, 1 on CPU2). This removes the concern that the first 45-second window was missed. We unloaded both modules, released the hold, verified LDO14 and touch clock were restored, and removed the staged files from the iPad RAM filesystem.

The Z1 runtime tests used the 12B410 firmware (`670eab55…` Z2FW). Orchard's J81 recipe pins a different Z2FW from iPadOS 15.8.8 (`b9b4a072…`). We generated that pinned Z2FW locally and verified its hash, then made the same Apple-order/proximity wrapper, keeping the source packet byte-for-byte. On a fresh driver load, firmware discovery reported **`0x382`**, proving that the newer image ran. Calibration status was again `0x4969` and `0x50` status frames appeared. During a second 35-second confirmed touch window, IRQ 57 stayed at its post-boot count and the input event device yielded no bytes. This rules out the older firmware image alone as the cause under the tested startup sequence.

The two archived `J81.mtprops` files have identical keys and 59,288-byte firmware packets. Their decoded firmware packets differ at only **four byte offsets** (`0x7427`, `0x744f`, `0xe495`, `0xe795`); the first two bytes change `0x81` to `0x82`, matching the discovered version change. This close similarity explains why changing IPSW versions was a useful control but is unlikely to address a missing board-level signal by itself.

## New lead: iOS 8.1 sets J81 operating mode after discovery

The original 12B410 iPad5,3 kernelcache has an `AppleMultitouchSPIJ82` personality for `multi-touch,j82` with `AutomaticallySetOperatingMode = true`. The 19H422 (15.8.8) personality omits this property. The older kext's method at `0xffffff8002fb94dc` reads that property and, when true, calls its report-write virtual method (`+0x6c0`) with report ID `0xab` and one data byte `0x00`. Its diagnostic strings are `Setting rOPERATING_MODE to 0x%02X` and `Will not modify rOPERATING_MODE`. The old J81 vtable has this method at `+0x598` (two vtable copies, file offsets `0xf61db0` and `0xf628f0`); the successful bootload handler calls `+0x598` after device discovery and before publishing ready. This is a concrete J81-specific post-discovery write that our current `apple_z2` path does not perform. The earlier N61 `0x9d`, `0xbf`, and `0xaf` experiments did not test it.

The method belongs to the older iOS 8.1 driver. The first test uses `0xab {0}` with matching 12B410 firmware (`0x381`); 19H422 (`0x382`) needs a separate test. A successful write acknowledgement alone does not establish that touch works; the test needs contact IRQs and input events during a confirmed finger window. The newer iOS driver may use a different operating-mode path, so failure with the newer firmware would not disprove the older trace.

An offline sweep of the archived iPad5,3 kernelcaches found `AutomaticallySetOperatingMode` in 12B410 and 12H321, but not in 13A344 or any later available build through 19H422. This is a whole-kernelcache string check, not proof of how newer iOS enables scanning; it places the removal of that named path between the archived iOS 8 and iOS 9 builds.

**Live report test:** The project's Linux builder guest could not reach SSH after a documented clean restart, so an isolated copy of the previously built module was changed at the single J81 `0xaf` report-ID instruction in `apple_z2_upload_firmware` (`.text+0x4d18`, ELF file offset `0x4eb8`). ARM64 instruction `12800a01` became `12800a81`, changing the helper's report ID from `0xaf` to `0xab`; a byte comparison confirmed exactly one byte differs from the original module, and disassembly confirmed the new immediate. With `j81_init=4`, the iPad booted the matching `0x381` firmware, discovered its surface, sent `e4 ab 01 00`, and received `e4 ab 00` (status zero). During a synchronized 60-second capture, the user confirmed dragging a finger for most of the window, but `/dev/input/event1` yielded **0 bytes** and IRQ 57 stayed at **8** (7 on CPU0, 1 on CPU2). The accepted operating-mode write is therefore **insufficient** to start contacts on this unit. We unloaded both modules, released the hold, confirmed LDO14 enable `0x00` and clock `0x800002ee`, and removed the test files from the iPad. The builder guest also shut down after failing to reach SSH; no tracked driver source was changed.

## Separate difference: display-sync timing

Apple enables `function-display_sync` between the `19 c1` wake and the 16-byte dummy transfer while reset is asserted (`research/2026-10-11-touch-no-ack-analysis.md`, steps 6–9). The decoded GPIO provider selects peripheral function 1 on GPIO55 (`research/2026-10-04-display-sync-pinmux-decode.md`). The current J81 driver omits this step. Runs Z1h–Z1j tried GPIO55 after boot, before the entire boot, and as a driven level; function 1 selected before power-up broke the boot handshake.

**Exact-window test, 2026-11-02:** With the touchscreen driver unloaded, we temporarily bound `spidev` to SPI3 and used the existing `touch-diag --apple-order --display-sync-pinmux` path. It sent wake `19 c1` while reset was held, waited 10 ms, muxed GPIO55 to function 1, sent the dummy, then released reset. Detect returned `1f 01`/`48 79` and **passed**. The matching 12B410 firmware packet ACKed `4b c1`; both touch-calibration chunks ACKed; five register writes ACKed `4a d1`; the calibration request returned `4969`; and execute produced **17 ATN edges in 2 seconds**, the same count as the earlier no-sync manual B1 run. Thus the exact timing is compatible with firmware boot. It does not establish whether it enables contacts, because this diagnostic does not perform runtime discovery or keep the controller running for a touch window.

While GPIO55 was muxed for that full boot, a separate read-only sampler saw `0x00072aa2` (sampled low) 602 times and `0x00072aa3` (sampled high) 594 times, with **137 observed level transitions** among 1,196 active samples. The display pipeline therefore supplies a changing signal on that pad. The sampler was not timed well enough to infer its frequency or duty cycle. The diagnostic restored GPIO55 to `0x00072a82`, LDO14 enable to `0x00`, and the clock to `0x800002ee`; temporary spidev and files were removed. A runtime touch test with GPIO55 enabled at that exact transition still requires a driver/pinctrl change.

## GPIO84 and report-drain check after outside feedback

The live J81 DT has `interrupts-extended = <GPIO-phandle 0x54 0x2>` (GPIO84, falling edge) and no `attn-gpios`. The shared driver's J81 entry uses the J71t variant. Its `apple_z2_n1_finish_boot()` calls the bounded `apple_z2_n1_service_locked()` **after discovery and before enabling the IRQ**, even without `attn-gpios`; absent that property, the drain stops when the report queue is empty. Thus the missing property does not by itself explain an already pending report at startup. Adding it may still help future runtime edge cases, but should follow a demonstrated low ATN line.

We reloaded the original (unpatched) module and 12B410 firmware. Before boot, `touch-diag status` showed GPIO84 config `0x0000038f`; after discovery it showed `0x0000038b`. In a synchronized 60-second window, with the user dragging for most of it, **all 60 one-second samples** read `0x0000038b`; IRQ 57 remained at 9 on CPU0 and 1 on CPU2. In the Apple GPIO driver, bit 0 of an input pin's word is the sampled data value; it stayed **high**. The line was neither held low nor producing an edge during touch. The touch clock remained ungated at `0x000802dc`. No `e1 56` length reply or runtime resynchronization appeared in `dmesg`. The `db_len == 0x7d` check mentioned in the J128 anecdote is in the J172-only initialization function, not the J81 path; J81 probe completed and remained bound. The transfer-alignment fix may matter once contact IRQs occur, but it does not explain this observed no-IRQ state.

An additional forced report read under the driver's lock would directly distinguish silent contact data from absent scanning, but the loaded module exposes no trigger for one. The current boot-time drain proves that report reads work for `0x50` status frames; it does not sample the queue during a later touch. A new diagnostic hook needs a rebuilt module. After the GPIO capture we unloaded the modules, released the hold, restored LDO14 enable `0x00` and the clock to `0x800002ee`, and removed the staged test files.

The five available module-boot transcripts on the still-running iPad all show the same two `0x50` frames immediately after discovery. The first has a little-endian trailing field of **10008** and a preceding flag field `0x0002`; about 144–149 ms later the second has **10148** (one run 10156) and flag `0x0006`. The counter delta tracks elapsed milliseconds, consistent with firmware timekeeping. No later `0x50` frame appears in those logs. This is evidence of two reproducible startup notifications, not evidence that electrode scans or contact reports are running. The old firmware's ARM image was decoded from its Z2 packet into a Ghidra scratch project; the report-get handler at offset `0x6f12` handles many IDs but has no `0x50` case, consistent with `0x50` being an unsolicited notification rather than a readable setting. Its exact flag meaning is still unknown.

## iOS 15 runtime power path resolves to three board functions

The local iOS 15 J81 `AppleMultitouchN1SPI` instance constructor (embedded `DTPlatformBuild=19H402`; source association with 19H422 verified; see [the provenance check](2026-11-08-j81-ios15-kernelcache-provenance.md)) installs vtable `0xfffffff006ddaaa0`, derived from `AppleMultitouchZ2SPI`'s `0xfffffff006dd9e88`. Its inherited `+0x718` power entry is `0xfffffff005de4414`, which schedules callback `+0x720` (`0xfffffff005de4448`) on the workloop. On a successful transition to a nonzero power state, that callback invokes the parent power function at `+0x728` (`0xfffffff005debd94`, then `0xfffffff005de41e8`), then the Z2SPI power-on hook at `+0x748` (`0xfffffff005debfb4`). The parent calls the ADT `function-power_mamba`, `function-power_ana`, and `function-power_ldo` providers as present. The Z2SPI hook calls, in order, `function-enable_cs` (GPIO51), `function-clock_enable` (PMGR touch clock), N1's overridden `+0x910` method (`0xfffffff005df1e84`), then `function-display_sync` (GPIO55). The N1 method transmits `19 c1` while reading two bytes and waits 10 ms. The board-function names come from the strings used by their cached provider lookups at `0xfffffff005dec5ac`, `0xfffffff005dec484`, and `0xfffffff005dec360`. On power-off, `+0x730` disables the board functions. This independently confirms the iOS 8 wake-to-display-sync order on iOS 15.

The current Linux setup holds LDO14 and the touch clock on and drives GPIO95 from the driver, but does not execute that full power-on hook: GPIO55 is normally left at its boot pinmux, and GPIO51 is owned by the SPI controller as a hardware chip select. Earlier experiments tried GPIO55 after discovery, before power-up, and during Apple's wake-to-dummy interval; none yet combined the exact interval with **runtime discovery and a confirmed contact window**. The new trace strengthens that one remaining test. It does not prove GPIO55 alone is the cause, because iOS also enables CS51 and its power-state transitions may differ. No new device report that unconditionally starts scanning appears in this power path.

## Scope and current boot

The iPad was booted with the J81 touch payload for these tests. After both captures, we unloaded `apple_z2` and `spi_apple`, released the `touch-diag` hold, and verified LDO14 enable returned to `0x00`, touch clock to `0x800002ee`, and only `gpio-keys` remained at `event0`. Test firmware and modules were removed from the iPad RAM filesystem. The J71t success cited in Orchard is the 2017 iPad 5 (`iPad6,11`), not the original iPad Air J71; its N1 bootloader is a useful comparison, but its board wiring and firmware differ. The working J172 iPad 7 touch driver is likewise a reference for lifecycle checks, not a drop-in J81 solution.

The previously suggested PMU `function-sensor_power` at `Lump 0x202` belongs to the **Oscar motion coprocessor** ADT node, not touch. Touch's `function-power_ana` is `Lump 0x20e`, already enabled during these tests. LDO14's stored `0x14` voltage code is the documented 6 V setting. Do not toggle the Oscar rail as a touch experiment. The useful next checks are an iOS bus/pin trace of GPIO55 and ATN during a real contact, or an isolated test of display-sync routing at Apple's exact wake-to-dummy transition.
